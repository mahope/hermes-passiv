Why Your Website Needs an Accessibility Statement

Since the European Accessibility Act (EAA) took effect in June 2025, every website offering goods or services to EU consumers must publish an accessibility statement. This is not optional — it is a legal requirement under Article 4 of the EAA, enforced by each EU member state's designated authority.

An accessibility statement serves three purposes:

Legal compliance

The EAA explicitly requires a published statement of accessibility conformance. Without one, your site is automatically non-compliant — regardless of how accessible it actually is.

User transparency

Visitors with disabilities can immediately understand your site's accessibility level, known issues, and how to report problems. This builds trust and reduces support friction.

Procurement requirement

Enterprise clients and marketplaces now ask for accessibility statements as part of vendor onboarding. Having one signals professionalism and pre-empts a common contract blocker.

The good news: an effective accessibility statement does not need to be long or legalistic. Most sites can write one in under an hour using the structure below — and our free generator does it in 2 minutes.

What the EAA Requires in an Accessibility Statement

Regulation (EU) 2023/2419 specifies exactly what every statement must include. The requirements are consistent across all member states that have transposed the directive:

Commitment to Accessibility

Requirement: A clear statement of your site's commitment to making content accessible to all users, regardless of ability.

Keep it short. One or two sentences stating your goal is sufficient.

Conformance Status

Requirement: The standard you aim to meet. For the EAA, this is WCAG 2.1 Level AA. You must state whether your site is fully conformant, partially conformant, or non-conformant.

Most sites are "partially conformant" — that is acceptable as long as known issues are documented.

Known Limitations

Requirement: A list of known accessibility issues and, where possible, an alternative way to access the content or service.

Be honest. Documented limitations are compliant. Hiding them is not.

Feedback Mechanism

Requirement: A way for users to report accessibility issues. This must include an email address or web form, and you must acknowledge receipt within a reasonable timeframe.

Recommended: email + phone + postal address. The more channels, the better.

Testing Methods

Requirement: Describe how you evaluated your site's accessibility — automated tools, manual testing, user testing, or external audit.

Even a self-assessment using our free scanner qualifies. Disclose the method honestly.

Enforcement Contact

Requirement: The contact details of the relevant national enforcement body. Users who are not satisfied with your response can escalate to this authority.

Each EU member state designates an enforcement body. Link to your country's authority.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Over the past year, we have reviewed dozens of agency client websites for EAA compliance. These are the most common statement pitfalls we see:

Claiming full conformance without evidence

If you claim "fully conformant WCAG 2.1 AA" and a user finds a contrast error or missing alt text, your statement becomes a liability. It is safer to claim "partially conformant" and document limitations honestly. You can upgrade to full conformance later when you have verified every page.

No feedback mechanism

A surprising number of statements describe the site's accessibility but provide no way for users to report issues. This alone can make the statement non-compliant. Always include at minimum an email address.

Outdated enforcement contact

Not all EU member states have designated their EAA enforcement body yet, and some changed their designated authority after the 2025 deadline. Check the European Commission's list before publishing. Our statement generator includes the correct contacts for each member state.

Hiding the statement

The statement must be easy to find from any page on your site — typically linked in the footer. Burying it in a privacy policy page or making it available only through search does not satisfy the "prominently displayed" requirement.

Outdated conformance reference

WCAG 2.2 was published in 2023, but the EAA references WCAG 2.1 AA specifically. Your statement should reference the correct standard (WCAG 2.1 AA) unless your jurisdiction explicitly updates the reference. Do not prematurely claim conformance to a standard the EAA does not require.

No date of last review

An accessibility statement must include the date it was last reviewed and updated. A statement that is two years old with no review date signals neglect and may be flagged in an audit. Review and re-date the statement quarterly when you run your accessibility scan.

Accessibility Statement Template

This is the minimum structure every EAA accessibility statement should follow. Paste it into a page on your site and fill in the placeholders:

Sample Structure

Accessibility Statement for [Your Site Name]

Commitment:
[Site Name] is committed to ensuring digital accessibility for people with disabilities. We continually improve the user experience for everyone and apply the relevant accessibility standards.

Conformance Status:
The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) define requirements for designers and developers to improve accessibility for people with disabilities. [Site Name] is partially conformant with WCAG 2.1 Level AA. Partially conformant means that some parts of the content do not fully conform to the accessibility standard.

Known Limitations:
Despite our best efforts, the following areas have known limitations:
- [List specific issues, e.g. "Older blog posts may contain PDFs that are not fully accessible"]
- [Alternative: "We are working to address [specific issue] by [date]"]

Feedback:
We welcome your feedback on the accessibility of [Site Name]. Please let us know if you encounter accessibility barriers:
- Email: [your email]
- Phone: [optional]
- Postal address: [optional]

Evaluation Methods:
We assessed the accessibility of [Site Name] using the following approach:
- Self-evaluation using automated WCAG scanning tools
- [Add: Manual testing with keyboard navigation]
- [Add: Screen reader testing if applicable]

Date:
This statement was created on [Date] and last reviewed on [Date].

Enforcement Body:
If you are not satisfied with our response, you may contact the relevant national authority:
[Your Country's EAA Enforcement Body Name]
[Website link]
[Postal address]

Accessibility Statement Checklist

Before publishing, verify each item:

Content

  • ✓ Commitment to accessibility stated
  • ✓ Conformance level declared (WCAG 2.1 AA)
  • ✓ Known limitations listed honestly
  • ✓ Feedback mechanism with email
  • ✓ Testing methods described
  • ✓ Enforcement contact included
  • ✓ Date of last review shown

Placement

  • ✓ Statement is on a public page (not login-gated)
  • ✓ Linked from every page footer
  • ✓ Page title includes "Accessibility Statement"
  • ✓ URL is intuitive (/accessibility or /accessibility-statement)

Maintenance

  • ✓ Scheduled quarterly review
  • ✓ Known limitations updated after each scan
  • ✓ Enforcement contact verified annually

Maintaining Your Accessibility Statement

An accessibility statement is not a one-time document. Treat it as a living record of your site's accessibility status:

  • Update after every scan. Run our free scanner quarterly (or after major site changes). Update the known limitations section to reflect what the scan found. A statement from 2025 that has not been touched since is a red flag in any procurement review.
  • Respond to feedback. If a user reports an issue through your feedback mechanism, acknowledge it within 48 hours and provide a reasonable timeline for a fix. Document the report and resolution in your accessibility log.
  • Review enforcement contacts annually. Member states update their designated authorities. The European Commission maintains an up-to-date list — check it once a year and update your statement if anything changed.
  • Update after site redesigns. A new theme, a CMS migration, or a major feature launch can change your site's accessibility. After the redesign is settled, run a full scan, update the known limitations section, and re-date the statement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does every page need its own statement?

No. One statement for the entire site is sufficient, as long as it covers the full scope of pages and services offered. Link it in the footer from every page.

What if I run a multisite network?

Each site needs its own statement unless all sites share the same theme and content management system. If each site has custom themes, plugins, or content, create separate statements. The statement generator supports multiple sites.

Can I use the same statement for multiple client sites?

No. Each site has different content, themes, and known limitations. Copying the same statement across client sites without customising the known limitations section is misleading and could be flagged in an audit.

Do I need to translate my accessibility statement?

If your site serves users in multiple EU countries, it is best practice — and increasingly required — to provide the statement in each language your site is available in. The statement itself is an essential accessibility feature that should be understandable to all users.

What happens if I do not have an accessibility statement?

Your site is automatically non-compliant with the EAA under Article 4. Enforcement varies by member state, but fines for non-compliance have already been issued across the EU (up to €900,000 in Sweden, €600,000 in Spain as of mid-2026). Beyond fines, you risk damaging your reputation and losing clients who require documented compliance.

Does the statement need to be on a separate page?

It must be on a dedicated page with an intuitive URL such as /accessibility or /accessibility-statement. A paragraph buried in a privacy policy or terms page does not satisfy the requirement. The URL should be linked from every page footer.
