The list shows levels A and AA (55 criteria). WCAG 2.2 additionally contains 31 AAA criteria, which are almost never required in full by law. The criterion numbers match W3C's official numbering, so you can look the details up directly at W3C.

Frequently asked questions

How many requirements are in WCAG 2.2?

WCAG 2.2 has 86 success criteria: 31 at level A, 24 at level AA and 31 at level AAA. Public bodies and most laws (including the EAA) require levels A and AA — 55 criteria in total.

What is new in WCAG 2.2 compared with 2.1?

Nine criteria were added in 2.2, six of them at level A or AA: focus not obscured (2.4.11), dragging alternatives (2.5.7), target size (2.5.8), consistent help (3.2.6), redundant entry (3.3.7) and accessible authentication (3.3.8). The other three are AAA-only, and 4.1.1 Parsing was removed. The ones that hit ordinary websites hardest are visible focus and login without cognitive traps.

Is WCAG 2.2 law in the EU?

Not directly. The EAA references EN 301 549, which incorporates WCAG 2.1 level A/AA — but market surveillance works with the newest version in practice, and the EU is updating the harmonised standards to 2.2. Build to WCAG 2.2 AA and you are covered for both.

What do levels A, AA and AAA mean?

The levels are grades of difficulty and importance. Level A is the basics (without them many people cannot use the page), AA is the standard law and contracts point to, and AAA is the best level — rarely required in full, because some AAA criteria cannot be met for all types of content.
