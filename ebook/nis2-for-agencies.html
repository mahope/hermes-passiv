Foreword

If you run a small web agency in the EU, this guide is for you.

NIS2 (the EU's Network and Information Security Directive 2) became enforceable in 2025. Unlike the original NIS directive, NIS2 casts a much wider net — it covers more sectors, more company sizes, and imposes stricter requirements. Most significantly for you: digital service providers, managed service providers, and their subcontractors are explicitly in scope.

This is not a legal document. It is a practical field guide written by someone who works with agencies like yours. Every recommendation has been tested with real agencies. You will not find abstract theory here — only what works.

Let's get you compliant.

Chapter 1: Does NIS2 Apply to Your Agency?

This is the first question, and it's the one most agencies get wrong. Many assume they are too small to be in scope. Under NIS2, that assumption can be costly.

The Size Test

NIS2 applies to medium-sized and large enterprises in covered sectors. The size thresholds:

  • Large enterprise: 250+ employees OR €50M+ annual turnover AND €43M+ balance sheet
  • Medium enterprise: 50–249 employees OR €10M–€50M turnover
  • Small enterprise: Below 50 employees AND below €10M turnover

Good news: If your agency has fewer than 50 employees and less than €10M annual turnover, you are a "small enterprise" and generally not directly in scope for NIS2.

Bad news: You still need to read this guide. Here's why.

Why Small Agencies Must Still Comply

  1. Your clients are in scope. If you serve medium or large enterprises that are subject to NIS2, they must assess their supply chain — and that includes you.
  2. Contractual requirements. Enterprise clients are already adding NIS2 clauses to vendor contracts. Without demonstrated compliance, you lose the contract.
  3. Downstream liability. Under NIS2 Article 21 on supply chain security, your clients must assess the security practices of their suppliers. If you are the weak link, they bear the penalty — and will pass liability to you.
  4. Competitive disadvantage. Agencies that demonstrate NIS2 alignment win more pitches.

Quick Self-Assessment

  1. Do any clients have more than 50 employees? → If yes, they are likely in NIS2 scope.
  2. Do you handle hosting, server management, DNS, email, SSL, backups, or security monitoring for clients? → If yes, you are a digital service provider in their supply chain.
  3. Do you have access to client networks, data, or administrative systems? → If yes, you are a vector they must secure.

If you answered yes to any of the above, you need to be NIS2-ready.

Chapter 2: The 10 Essential Security Measures (Article 21)

Article 21 requires "appropriate and proportionate technical, operational, and organisational measures." Here is each category translated for small agencies.

2.1 Risk Analysis and Security Policies

What to do: Write a one-page Information Security Policy covering acceptable use, password requirements, device security, and remote work rules. Review it annually. Store it where all employees can access it.

2.2 Incident Handling

What to do: Designate a Security Contact. Create a 6-step Incident Response Plan: Detect → Triage → Contain → Eradicate → Recover → Review. Use a free monitoring tool (UptimeRobot, Better Stack). For WordPress agencies: install Wordfence or iThemes Security with real-time monitoring.

2.3 Business Continuity and Backup

What to do: Implement the 3-2-1 backup rule: 3 copies, 2 different media, 1 offsite. Use UpdraftPlus or BlogVault for automated backups. Test restoration quarterly. Document your backup procedure.

2.4 Supply Chain Security

What to do: List every third-party service. For each, document: what data they handle, their certifications (SOC 2, ISO 27001), and data processing location. Require subcontractors to meet equivalent standards.

2.5 System Acquisition and Maintenance

What to do: Keep everything updated. Remove unused plugins and themes. Weekly security patches, monthly full reviews.

2.6 Vulnerability Handling

What to do: Subscribe to security advisories for all software you use. Use WPScan for WordPress vulnerabilities. Create security@yourdomain.com for disclosures.

2.7 Testing and Auditing

What to do: Run an automated security scan quarterly (HackerTarget, Detectify, WPScan). Review access logs monthly. Document all tests.

2.8 Cryptography and Encryption

What to do: HTTPS everywhere (LetsEncrypt is free). Encrypt all backups. Use a password manager (Bitwarden, 1Password). Enable MFA on all admin accounts.

2.9 Human Resources Security

What to do: New hire security briefing (30 min: passwords, phishing, data handling). Annual refresher. Confidentiality clauses in contracts.

2.10 Access Control

What to do: Least privilege. Role-based access. Quarterly access review. Remove former employees' accounts immediately. Use a user role editor for WordPress.

Chapter 3: Incident Reporting

NIS2 imposes strict timelines: 24 hours for early warning, 72 hours for full notification.

What Qualifies as Reportable

  • Intrusion into client hosting environment
  • Ransomware on your systems
  • Data breach involving client data
  • Extended outage from a security event
  • Compromise of client admin accounts

The Timeline

TimeAction
Within 24hEarly warning to client security contact
Within 72hDetailed notification (what, impact, risks)
Upon resolutionFinal report (root cause, actions, prevention)

Early Warning Template (24h)

To: [Client Security Contact]
Subject: Security Incident Notification — [Brief Description]

We are writing to inform you of a security incident affecting [systems/services].

Status: Ongoing / Contained / Resolved
Initial impact: [Brief description]
Current actions: [What we are doing]
Next update: [Date/time]

Contact: [Your name], [Your email/phone]

What Not to Do

  • Do not assume you are "too small to report" — under-reporting carries penalties.
  • Do not wait for full clarity before the early warning.
  • Do not report without preserving evidence (logs, screenshots, timestamps).

Chapter 4: Supply Chain Security

This is the chapter that matters most. Under NIS2, your clients must assess you.

What Clients Will Ask

  1. Do you have a documented Information Security Policy?
  2. What backup procedures do you follow?
  3. Are you certified under ISO 27001, SOC 2, or equivalent?
  4. Where is client data stored and processed?
  5. What access controls do you have?
  6. Do you conduct regular security testing?
  7. How do you handle incidents?
  8. Do you conduct background checks on employees?
  9. Do you use subcontractors handling client data?
  10. What is your password policy?

Build a Vendor Security Profile

Create a single PDF answering all 10 questions. Update annually. Share proactively in pitches — it signals professionalism.

Chapter 5: Contract Clauses for NIS2

Add these to your client contracts.

Security Obligations

The Provider shall maintain appropriate technical and organisational security measures to protect the Client's data and systems, including but not limited to: access controls, encryption in transit and at rest, regular security updates, and backup procedures.

Incident Notification

The Provider shall notify the Client within 24 hours of becoming aware of any security incident affecting the Client's data or systems, and shall provide a full incident report within 72 hours, including root cause analysis and remediation steps.

Subcontractor Security

The Provider shall ensure that any subcontractor or third-party service provider with access to Client data or systems maintains security measures equivalent to those described in this agreement.

Data Processing

The Provider shall process Client data only in accordance with the Client's documented instructions. Data shall be stored and processed within the European Economic Area unless otherwise agreed.

Compliance Verification

Upon the Client's reasonable request (not more than once per 12-month period), the Provider shall complete a security questionnaire or provide documentary evidence of implemented security measures.

Version: 1.0 | Date: [DATE]

1. Purpose: This policy defines security requirements for all employees, contractors, and systems.

2. Scope: All employees, contractors, systems, and data.

3. Policy:
a) Access Control: Least-privilege basis. Quarterly reviews. Accounts terminated within 24h of departure.
b) Authentication: MFA required on all administrative systems. Passwords: 16+ characters, stored in Bitwarden.
c) Data Protection: Client data stored within EU. All data encrypted in transit (TLS 1.2+). Backups encrypted at rest.
d) Incident Response: Report to [CONTACT] within 1 hour. See Incident Response Plan.
e) Software Updates: Security patches within 7 days of release.
f) Third Parties: Vendors with system access must meet equivalent security standards.

4. Review: Annually by [ROLE/TITLE].

Step 1: Detect — Alerts, user reports, client reports.

Step 2: Triage (within 15 min) — Severity (Low/Med/High/Critical). Assign response lead.

Step 3: Contain — Isolate systems. Preserve evidence. Notify clients (within 24h).

Step 4: Eradicate — Remove cause. Apply patches. Reset credentials.

Step 5: Recover — Restore from backup. Verify integrity. Monitor 48h.

Step 6: Review (within 7 days) — Root cause analysis. Update procedures.

Final Words

NIS2 compliance for a small web agency is not about building a Fort Knox. It is about being organized, documented, and transparent.

The agencies that thrive under NIS2 are not the ones with the biggest security budgets. They are the ones that can show clients: "Here is what we do, here is how we do it, here is proof it works."

Start today. Follow the 30-day checklist. You do not need a compliance team. You just need one afternoon and this guide.
